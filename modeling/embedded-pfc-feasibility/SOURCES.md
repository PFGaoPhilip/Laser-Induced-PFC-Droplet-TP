# Sources and claim boundaries

Primary and official sources were checked on 2026-10-11. Calculated comparison values in this project are original conditional results, not measurements from these sources.

| ID | Source | What it supports; what it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Abeid, Fabiilli, Aliabouzar and Estrada (2024), [Experimental & numerical investigations of ultra-high-speed dynamics of optically induced droplet cavitation in soft materials](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmbbm.2024.106776); [author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12557251/) | Experiments with PFC droplets embedded in fibrin, polyacrylamide and gelatin establish physical feasibility of optically triggered droplet cavitation. PFP/PFH/PFO and 3/6/12 µm **diameters** were compared. The apparatus uses single 532 nm, 4 ns pulses; its quoted 1.3–1.8 mJ apparatus pulse energy is not absorbed energy in one droplet. The associated bubble model includes water vapor and noncondensable gas. This is not validation of this project's PFC-only equilibrium control, an exterior jet, or the user's formulation. |
| S2 | Gao et al. (2021), [Equations of State for the Thermodynamic Properties of n-Perfluorobutane, n-Perfluoropentane, and n-Perfluorohexane](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.1c02969); [official PFP implementation](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/n-Perfluoropentane.html), [PFH implementation](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/n-Perfluorohexane.html) | Helmholtz real-fluid EOS used for pressure, internal energy and chemical potential. Archived CoolProp 8.0.0 coefficient JSONs are bundled. EOS functions do not supply optical absorption, nucleation rates, gel properties, fracture energy, or an interface law. |
| S3 | [NIST Chemistry WebBook, perfluoropentane](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C678262&Mask=4) | Molecular mass 288.0343 g/mol; compiled boiling temperatures 302.6–303.2 K; standard vaporization enthalpy entries 26.6 and 27.5 kJ/mol. These are bulk reference quantities, not nanodroplet activation temperatures or constant latent heats for every modeled state. |
| S4 | Kandadai (2010), [Rheology and interfacial tension of biopolymers, author dissertation](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/dl_files/40/6e/406e61ce35554485df3ea659a68ff430277482b7.pdf), printed pp. 113–119, Tables 5.2–5.3; [related article](https://doi.org/10.1021/la100307r) | Room-temperature PFP/saturated-air tension 9.41 mN/m and PFP/distilled-water tension 54 mN/m. Used as distinct inner/outer constant controls. Neither is measured high-temperature PFP/gel surface stress. |
| S5 | [Acoustic Droplet Vaporization of Perfluorohexane Emulsions Induced by Heterogeneous Nucleation at 1.1 MHz](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.3c02272), Table 2 and nucleation discussion | PFH liquid/gas reference tension 12 ± 2 mN/m at 20 °C; emphasizes distinct interfaces and heterogeneous nucleation. PFH uses the same **stipulated** 54 mN/m outer tension as PFP here to make a controlled EOS comparison; that assignment is not a PFH/gel measurement. |
| S6 | Luo et al. (2020), [Laser cavitation rheology for measurement of elastic moduli and failure strain within hydrogels](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-68621-y) | Bubble-radius measurements combined with a viscoelastic bubble model can identify modulus and failure strains. Supports measuring dynamic properties in the relevant regime. It does not justify equating a target gel's quasi-static modulus with high-rate properties or calling stretch 2.1 universal rupture. |
| S7 | Zhao et al. (2023), [Factors Influencing the Repeated Transient Optical Droplet Vaporization Threshold and Lifetimes of Phase Change, Perfluorocarbon Nanodroplets](https://doi.org/10.3390/nano13152238) | Optical activation depends on formulation and exposure. The earlier project's approximate 18.6 mJ/cm² threshold concerns a conditioned population with about 309 nm **diameter** at 0 °C, 1064 nm/5 ns and PAAm. It cannot calibrate a single unconditioned 300 nm-**radius** inclusion at room temperature. |
| S8 | Brennen (1995), [Cavitation and Bubble Dynamics, Chapter 2](https://media.library.caltech.edu/CaltechBOOK:1995.001/chap2.htm) | Spherical inertia, bubble thermodynamics and limits of incompressible bubble descriptions. A spherical equation cannot produce a directional collapse jet or a resolved shock-pressure history. |
| S9 | COMSOL 6.4, [Damage](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_solid.07.040.html), [Damage Models](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_theory.06.036.html) | Hyperelastic phase-field damage and fracture-energy/length-scale inputs exist in appropriate licensed products. A damage field alone does not define an open, fluid-filled hydraulic path. |
| S10 | COMSOL 6.4, [Mixed-Mode Debonding of a Laminated Composite](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.sme.cohesive_zone_debonding/cohesive_zone_debonding.html) | Official cohesive traction–separation example and Adhesion/Decohesion setup under Interior Contact. It is a software implementation reference, not a gel-specific calibration or validation. |
